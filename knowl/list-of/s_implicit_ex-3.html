Exercise 3.

Find the tangent line to the curve \(x^{3}+y^{3}=6xy\) at the point \((3,3)\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
\begin{equation*} x^{3}+(y(x))^{3}=6xy(x) \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} \dfrac{d}{dx}[x^{3}+(y(x))^{3}]=\dfrac{d}{dx}[6xy(x)] \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} 3x^{2}+3y^{2}y'=6y+6xy' \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} 3y^{2}y'-6xy'=6y-3x^{2} \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} (3y^{2}-6x)y'=6y-3x^{2} \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} y'=\dfrac{6x-3x^2}{3y^2-6x}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Thus, the slope of the tangent line at \((3,3)\) is
\begin{equation*} m=\dfrac{6\cdot 3-3\cdot 3^2}{3\cdot3^2-6\cdot 3}=-1. \end{equation*}
Therefore, the equation of the tangent line is \(y-3=-1(x-3)\) or \(y=-x+6\text{.}\)
