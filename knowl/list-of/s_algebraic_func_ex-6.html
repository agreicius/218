Exercise 6.

Find the (real) roots of the following polynomials.
  1. \(\displaystyle x^{3}-2x^{2}-x+2\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. \(\displaystyle x^{3}-4x^{2}+x+6\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. \(\displaystyle x^{4}+x^{3}+2x-4\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Solution.
  1. \(x^{3}-2x^{2}-x+2=0 \iff x^{2}(x-2)-(x-2)=0\iff (x-2)(x^{2}-1)=0\iff (x-2)(x-1)(x+1)=0\) and therefore, the zeroes are \(2,1,-1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. This polynomial does not factor as easily as the previous one. Using the Integer Root theorem, the possible integer solutions to the equation \(x^{3}-4x^{2}+x+6=0\) are the divisors of \(6\text{,}\) i.e., \(\pm1,\pm2,\pm3,\pm6\text{.}\) Notice that \(-1\) is one solution. Then use long division of polynomials to divide the given polynomial by \(x-(-1)=x+1\text{:}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \begin{array}{r@{}l}& \underline{x^2-5x+6} \\ x+1\,\big)\,& x^3-4x^2+x+6 \\[1mm] & \underline{x^3+x^2} \\[-1mm] & \phantom{x^3+}(-5x^2+x) \\[1mm] & \underline{\phantom{x^3+}(-5x^2-5x)} \\[-1mm] & \phantom{x^3-4x^2+}(6x+6) \\[1mm] & \underline{\phantom{x^3-4x^2+}(6x+6)} \\[-1mm] & 0\end{array}. \end{equation*}
    Next, we solve the equation \(x^{2}-5x+6=0\iff(x-3)(x-2)=0\iff x=3,x=2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Thus, the roots of the polynomial are \(-1,2,3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Similarly, we notice that 1 is a solution to the equation \(x^{4}+x^{3}+2x-4=0\text{.}\) We use long division to divide the given polynomial by \(x-1\text{:}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \begin{array}{r@{}l}& \underline{x^3+2x^2+2x+4} \\ x-1\,\big)\,& x^4+x^3+0x^2+2x-4 \\[1mm] & \underline{x^4-x^3} \\[-1mm] & \phantom{x^4+}(2x^3+0x^2) \\[1mm] & \underline{\phantom{x^4+}(2x^3-2x^2)} \\[-1mm] & \phantom{x^4+x^3+}(2x^2+2x) \\[1mm] & \underline{\phantom{x^4+x^3+}(2x^2-2x)} \\[-1mm] & \phantom{x^4+x^3+0x^2+}(4x-4) \\[1mm] & \underline{\phantom{x^4+x^3+0x^2+}(4x-4)} \\[-1mm] & 0\end{array}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Next, we solve the equation \(x^{3}+2x^{2}+2x+4=0 \iff x^{2}(x+2)+2(x+2)=0\iff (x^{2}+2)(x+2)=0\text{.}\) Since \(x^{2}+2=0\) has no real solutions, the only roots of the polynomial are 1 and -2.
    🔗
    Note that if \(x^{3}+2x^{2}+2x+4=0\) was not easily factorizable, we would use the integer root theorem and long division, as previously.
