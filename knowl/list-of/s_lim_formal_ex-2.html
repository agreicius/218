Exercise 2.

Let \(\epsilon=1\text{.}\) Find a \(\delta>0\) that satisfies the \(\epsilon-\delta\) definition of the limit \(\displaystyle{\lim_{x\to2} (4x-1)}=7\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
Using the notation \(\displaystyle{\lim_{x \to a} f(x)}=L\text{,}\) we have \(f(x)=4x-1,a=2,L=7\text{.}\)
\begin{equation*} |f(x)-L|<\epsilon \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} |4x-1-7|<1 \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} |4x-8|<1 \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} |4(x-2)|<1 \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} 4|x-2|<1 \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} |x-2|<0.25 \end{equation*}
🔗
Thus, any \(\delta <0.25\) satisfies the \(\epsilon-\delta\) definition of the given limit.
