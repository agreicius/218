Exercise 3.

Let \(\epsilon=1\text{.}\) Find a \(\delta>0\) that satisfies the \(\epsilon-\delta\) definition of the limit \(\displaystyle{\lim_{x\to-1} (x^2-2x)}=3\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
Using the notation \(\displaystyle{\lim_{x \to a} f(x)}=L\text{,}\) we have \(f(x)=x^{2}-2x,a=-1,L=3\text{.}\)
\begin{equation*} |f(x)-L|<\epsilon \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} |x^{2}-2x-3|<1 \end{equation*}
