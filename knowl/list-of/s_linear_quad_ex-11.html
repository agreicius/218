Exercise 11.

The bisector of a line segment is a line that passes through the midpoint of the line segment and is perpendicular to it. Find an equation of the bisector of the line segment that connects the points \((-3,-5)\) and \((1,1)\text{.}\) Write the equation in the form \(y=ax+b\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
The midpoint of the line segment has coordinates \(\left(\dfrac{-3+1}{2},\dfrac{-5+1}{2}\right)=(-1,-2)\text{.}\) Since the bisector is perpendicular to the line segment, \(m_{1}\cdot m_{2}=-1\) holds for their respective slopes. The slope of the line segment is \(m_{1}=\dfrac{1-(-5)}{1-(-3)}=\dfrac{3}{2}\text{.}\) Therefore, we find the slope \(m_{2}\) of the line by solving the equation \(\dfrac{3}{2}\cdot m_{2}=-1\text{.}\) Hence, \(m_{2}=-\dfrac{2}{3}\text{,}\) so the equation of the line is \(y-(-2)=-\dfrac{2}{3}(x-(-1))\text{,}\) or \(y=-\dfrac{2}{3}x-\dfrac{8}{3}\text{.}\)
