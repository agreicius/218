Exercise 4.

Find the point(s) on the curve \(x^{2}-xy+y^{2}=3\) where the tangent line is horizontal.
🔗
Solution.
The tangent line is horizontal at points where \(y'(x)=0\text{.}\)
\begin{equation*} x^{2}-xy(x)+(y(x))^{2}=3 \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} \dfrac{d}{dx}[x^{2}-xy(x)+(y(x))^{2}]=\dfrac{d}{dx}[3] \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} 2x-(y(x)+xy'(x))+2y(x)y'(x)=0 \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} (-x+2y)y'=-2x+ \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} y'=\dfrac{-2x+y}{-x+2y} \end{equation*}
Thus, \(y'=0\) when \(\dfrac{-2x+y}{-x+2y}=0\) i.e., when \(-2x+y\) or \(y=2x\text{.}\) Substituting into the equation of the curve, we get
\begin{equation*} x^{2}-2x^{2}+4x^{2}=3 \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} 3x^{2}=3 \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} x^{2}=1 \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} x=1\text{ or }x=-1. \end{equation*}
Therefore, the points on the curve where the tangent is horizontal is \((1,2)\) and \((-1,-2)\text{.}\)
